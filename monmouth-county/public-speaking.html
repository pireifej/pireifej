Whole-Room Drill

The Body Speaks First: Vocal & Physical Reset

Reset gently. Stay within your comfortable range. Never force or strain.

01

Neck, Jaw & Shoulders

Gently release tension. Keep your neck movements easy.

02

Motor Resonance

Try lip trills or a soft motorboat buzz. Keep airflow steady.

03

Forward Placement

Hum an easy “mmm-mmm.” Feel it forward; don’t push your throat.

Teaching Block

Perfection is the Enemy of Presence

Stanford’s “Dare to be Dull” reframe gives you permission to stop auditioning for brilliance.

The brilliant-thing trap

Trying to impress can freeze your mind. You lose the thread and stop listening.

Dare to be useful

Drop the perfect answer. Offer one honest, useful thought. Connect instead of performing.

Exercise 1A · Pairs

The Energy Drain: “Yes, But...” vs. “Yes, And...”

Turn to your neighbor. Choose Person A and Person B. For 60 seconds, plan an event using strictly “Yes, but...”

1. Propose

Person A offers an event idea.

2. Block politely

Person B begins every reply: “Yes, but...”

3. Notice

Where does momentum stall? What happens to your energy?

60 seconds

Polite objection can stall momentum, trigger defensiveness, and create conversational deadlock.

Exercise 1B · Whole Room

The Penguin Protocol: Group “Yes, And...”

“A colony of penguins just took over the corporate breakroom.”

Accept

Treat the prior sentence as unquestioned fact.

Add

Add one sentence beginning with “Yes, and...”

Pass

Send the story to the next volunteer.

Key lesson: absurd premises can quiet analytical self-censorship and level the room.

Accept the Offer

Build the Bridge

Validate their reality before you steer toward an outcome.

Part Two

The Rapid-Thinking Framework

Structure sets you free: instant containers for real-time clarity

Core Framework

The PREP Model for Spontaneous Clarity

P

Point

Lead with the bottom line in one sentence.

R

Reason

Name the fundamental why or strategic rationale.

E

Evidence / Story

Give one concrete fact, metric, or real-life example.

P

Point Action

Reiterate the conclusion with a clear call to action.

Exercise 2 · Partner Sprint
45SECONDS
ZERO ADVANCE PREP

The 45-Second PREP Sprint

Rapid turn-and-talk with your neighbor. Partner A gets a surprise prompt and delivers Point, Reason, Evidence, Point. Then switch roles with a new prompt.

Example surprise prompts: “Should meetings be 25 minutes?” · “What is one process we should simplify?”
Live Facilitator Coaching

The Hot-Seat: Handling the Interruption

One volunteer takes the mic. We practice the protocol under a little pressure.

1

Take a 2-second breath. Replace panic fillers with confident silence.

2

Acknowledge the interruption. Start with “Yes, and...” to show you heard the offer.

3

Anchor back. Return to a tight PREP conclusion.

Executive Presence Insight
2.0s

The micro-pause

The 2.0-Second Micro-Pause

Dead air can feel like an eternity to the speaker. To the listener, a short pause can signal composure, authority, and confidence.

Try it before your next answer. Let the thought land, then speak.
