CENTER-STAGE LAB · 8 MINUTES

Cross-division standoff.

The Accountable Negotiation Protocol

PERSON A · REQUESTER

[Specific Item] + [Hard Deadline] + [External Reason]

“I need Project X by 4:00 PM because leadership has a mandatory briefing at 9:00 AM tomorrow.”

Eliminates vague panic & rank-pulling.

PERSON B · RECEIVER

[Locked Commitment] + [Counter-Offer Option] + [Check-In]

“I'm locked on Task Y until 3:00 PM. I can review the top 3 items today, or the full file by 10:00 AM tomorrow. Does that work?”

Replaces the brick wall with a viable option.

THE ROOM · OBSERVERS

[Check Reason] + [Check Constraint] + [Check Agreement]

  • Did you hear an operational reason, not just raw urgency?
  • Did you hear a real constraint with an alternative option?
  • Did they lock in a mutual next step?

1. TEST THE SYSTEM

Don’t diagnose the person.

  • Is there chronic overload or burnout?
  • Is role scope or ownership genuinely undefined?
  • Is there an unspoken upstream dependency blocking them?
  • Is there a clear priority mismatch between units?

Facilitator notes · 60 minutes

1 · 3 min — Welcome

Paul Ireifej opens with practical accountability without humiliation. Set a no-names, no-case-details boundary.

2 · 4 min — Leadership mirror

Ask what staff can reasonably infer from visible signals. Do not supply personal stories or office examples.

3 · 5 min — Diagnostic

Run both poll steps: friction first, morale leakage second. Upload the local QR image before delivery; it remains in browser memory.

4 · 4 min — Inner circle

Name apparent favoritism as a process risk, not an accusation. Focus on visible decision paths.

5 · 4 min — Earshot radius

Contrast public-floor work awareness with private social coordination. Keep examples hypothetical.

6 · 5 min — Intentional footprint

Read the three 30-second contacts aloud. Ask participants to choose wording that sounds natural in their role.

7 · 8 min — Standoff lab

Assign fictional requester and receiver roles. Require each to state an operational need, constraint, and next step; no actual personnel examples.

8 · 5 min — Trade-off

Move the room from rank-based positions to a shared agency priority. Capture only generic work patterns.

9 · 4 min — Resistance

Do not diagnose any actual person. Use burnout and role clarity as possible work conditions to test, alongside defiance as only one possibility.

10 · 6 min — Email lab

Toggle A and B. Have pairs identify blame language in A, then the firm owner/timing/process commitments in B. Keep the email fictional.

11 · 4 min — Megaphone

Press M when no one is typing, enter a room-approved fictional rewrite, then clear it before leaving. Live text stays only in memory.

12 · 3 min — Guardrails

State the strict Zero-AI instruction. Confirm exact office policy wording with leadership before presenting; do not characterize it as verified policy.

13 · 5 min — Challenge

Invite one private commitment and start the 60-second reflection timer. The QR is intentionally unconfigured for future job aids and 1-on-1 templates.
