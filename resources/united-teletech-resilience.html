01 / The useful version

Four frames for the messy middle.

Change gets easier to work with when we can name what is happening. Keep these ideas close during the rollout.

01 · BRIDGES

Ending → Neutral Zone → New Beginning

William Bridges separates a transition (the human process) from a change (the external event). We first let go of familiar ways, move through an uncertain learning period, and eventually build a new beginning. A temporary productivity dip is normal: attention is split between the work and learning the new way.

the dip
02 · COGNITIVE FRICTION

New takes more attention.

Under stress, familiar routines often feel faster. New workflows ask us to notice, remember, choose, and check more than usual; that added cognitive load can make small friction feel big. This is a careful, plain-language description of change-related cognitive friction—not a claim that Prosci originated neuroscience. Slow down enough to get the next useful fact.

03 · CONTROL / INFLUENCE / ACCEPT

Put your energy where it can work.

Use Control for your own actions and preparation; Influence for what you can shape through questions, handoffs, and clear feedback; Accept for realities you cannot change today. Paul credits Dr. Terry Paulson with the workshop’s practical three-bucket change-leadership framing. Its broader conceptual foundation aligns with Stephen Covey’s Circle of Influence—not as an identical model or a claim of origin, but as a compatible way to choose agency.

04 · LUCY HONE

Helping or harming?

When frustration spikes, ask: “Is what I’m doing helping or harming me?” The question is a circuit breaker, not a demand to feel positive. It creates a beat to choose a response that supports you, your teammate, or the member in front of you.

When the system pauses, slow the conversation down. Use these interpersonal prompts while following all official United Teletech Credit Union procedures.

1. Acknowledge

Name the impact without blaming the member, a teammate, or the technology. Try: “I can see this delay is frustrating. Thank you for your patience while I check the next step.”

2. Clarify

Share only what is known and appropriate. Avoid guessing, overpromising, or exposing internal details. Try: “I want to make sure this is handled accurately. Let me verify the approved process.”

3. Connect

Explain the immediate next action and use the credit union’s official fallback or escalation channel. Try: “Here is what I can do now, and when you can expect the next update.”

A 10-second circuit breaker

“Is what I’m doing helping or harming this interaction?”

  • Helpful: a calm pace, plain language, accurate expectations, active listening, and a clear handoff through approved channels.
  • Pause before: speculating about causes, promising an exact resolution time, sharing internal or sensitive details, or inventing a workaround.

Control: your tone and next action. Influence: the quality of the handoff. Accept: what is outside your authority.

Important: This is general interpersonal de-escalation and communication guidance. It does not replace or override United Teletech Credit Union operational, security, transaction, escalation, or compliance policies. Always follow official internal procedures.
