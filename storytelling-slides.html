10 Minutes

Physical & Vocal Warm-Up

Why Warm Up?

Athletes stretch before a game. Singers warm up before a concert.

Your voice and body are your instruments — treat them that way!
Warm-Up 1

The “Big Stretch”

Reach for the ceiling on tip-toes, stretching as tall as you can.

Then drop and hang like a ragdoll, arms dangling loose. Shake it out!

Warm-Up 2

The “Accordion” Breath

1

Put your hands on your ribs.

2

Inhale for 4 counts (feel your ribs expand outward).

3

Hold for 4 counts.

4

Exhale for 8 counts on a long “hisssss.”

This deep rib-expansion breath powers your voice!
Warm-Up 3

The Vocal Siren

Start at your lowest comfortable pitch on an “ooo” or “aaa” sound and slide smoothly to your highest note and back down, like a fire truck.

Wheeee-ooooo!

This stretches the vocal cords and increases range.

10 Minutes

The Anatomy of a Personal Story

The Big Idea

A story isn’t just a list of things that happened — it needs a “heart.”

“The audience doesn’t remember what you said. They remember how you made them FEEL.”
Story Structure

Plot Diagram

Story Part 1

The Hook

Drop us into the action immediately.

Don’t start with “So one day...” — start with a moment that grabs attention!

Story Part 2

The Conflict

The “uh-oh” moment.

What went wrong? What was scary, hard, or unexpected?

This is the heart of every story.
Story Part 3

The Resolution

How you fixed it — or what you learned.

The audience should walk away with something meaningful.
Example

Hook vs. No Hook

Weak Start

“So, um, one day last summer my family went on vacation and we went to the beach and it was really fun...”

Strong Hook

“The wave hit me so hard I didn’t know which way was up. My feet couldn’t find the sand. And the only thing I could think was — I should have listened to my mom.”

Crafting Exercise

Share Time

After drafting, volunteers stand up and share their 3-sentence story with the class using their Level 5 volume.

Audience feedback: “What was the heart of their story?”

Pro Tip: The best stories aren’t about the biggest events — they’re about the smallest moments that taught you something real.
5 Minutes

Closing: The Hero’s Pose

The Power Pose

Stand up tall. Feet shoulder-width apart. Hands on hips or fists raised above your head like a superhero. Hold for 30 seconds.

Research shows that “power posing” for just 30 seconds can make you feel more confident before you speak. End every session feeling like a hero!

“You are the hero of your own story. Stand like it. Speak like it. Own it.”

“1, 2, 3... HERO POSE!”
