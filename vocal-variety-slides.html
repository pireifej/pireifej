Physical & Vocal Warm-Up

Get Ready

Warm-Up

Stretch & Breathe

The "Big Stretch"

Reach your arms high above your head, then hang low and touch your toes. Loosen the instrument — your body!

The "Accordion" Breath

Put your hands on your ribs. Breathe in deeply and feel your ribs expand outward like an accordion. This deep rib-expansion breath powers your voice!

Warm-Up

Siren & Lip Trills

The Vocal Siren

Slide your voice from your lowest note alllll the way to your highest, like a fire truck siren. Wheeee-ooooo! This stretches your vocal range.

Raspberries & Lip Trills

Brrrrrrr! Blow raspberries to loosen up your lips and get the vibrations going. Then try humming while you do it.

Warm-Up

The "Volume Dial"

Vocal Variety

Your Voice Has Levels

Change volume, pace, and character to make the audience feel the story.

Don't just tell it — perform it!
Volume

Whisper to Megaphone

The Whisper (Level 1)

When do you whisper? Secrets, scary moments, surprises.

Whispering actually makes the audience lean in closer to listen. It pulls them into your world.

Try it: "I opened the door slowly... and what I saw... changed everything."
Volume

The Megaphone

The Megaphone (Level 5)

When do you yell? Excitement, anger, cheering!

Use this when you reach the "Boss Battle" or the climax of your story. It wakes the audience up!

Try it: "And the crowd went WILD!"
Pace

The Speed Pedal

The Cheetah (Fast)

Use for action! "I grabbed the controller, I hit the button, the screen flashed, and BOOM!" Fast speech makes the audience's heart beat faster.

The Turtle (Slow)

Use for serious moments, or right before a big reveal. "I opened the door... and there it was." Pausing and speaking slowly builds suspense.

Voices

The Character Switch

You are the narrator of your speech. But sometimes, other characters talk! Change your posture and voice to play a different character for just one sentence.

Example: "My mom put her hands on her hips and said, [strict adult voice] 'Clean your room!'"
"A great speaker doesn't just tell you the story. They make you FEEL it — through volume, speed, and character!"

15 Minutes

Body Language

Base Camp (The Resting Position)

When you aren't doing anything in a game, your character stands in their "idle animation." Your idle animation is Base Camp.

  • Feet shoulder-width apart
  • Hands drop totally relaxed to the side
  • No pockets. No crossing arms. No wringing hands.
The Rule: "If you don't know what to do with your hands, send them to Base Camp."
Your Hands Are Tools

The 3 Tool Gestures

Your hands are tools to help the audience see the words!

The Number Tool

If you say "I have three reasons," you must hold up 3 fingers!

The Size Tool

If something is tiny, pinch your fingers. If it is massive, stretch your arms as wide as they go!

The Heart Tool

If you are talking about yourself, your feelings, or your opinion, bring one hand to your chest.

Stage Movement

"Walk and Plant"

The Mistake: "The Tiger in the Cage" — pacing back and forth nervously without stopping.

The Fix: "Walk on a period." Stand in the center for your first point. When you transition to your next point, take three steps to the left, plant your feet like tree roots, and stay there.

The Golden Rule: Never speak while your feet are moving aimlessly.
"Your hands and face speak just as loudly as your voice. Make every movement intentional!"
Exercise • 10 Minutes

The Silent Storyteller

The Challenge

Act out a scenario for 30 seconds without making a single sound.

The group guesses what you're doing!
Silent Storyteller

Scenarios 1-5

"Your favorite sports team just won the championship and you're celebrating!"

"You're opening a birthday present and it's exactly what you wanted!"

"There's a spider on your shoulder and you just noticed it."

"You're eating ice cream and it falls off the cone onto the ground."

"You're shooting the game-winning basketball shot in slow motion."

Silent Storyteller

Scenarios 6-10

"You're trying to give a cat a bath and it does NOT want one."

"You just bit into the spiciest food you've ever tasted."

"Your alarm goes off for school and you really don't want to get up."

"You're playing a video game and you just beat the final boss."

"You look out the window and it just started snowing — no school today!"

Discussion After Each Turn: "What did they do with their hands or face that helped you understand the story?"
